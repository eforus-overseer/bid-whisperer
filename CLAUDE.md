# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for AI assistants and humans working in this repository.

## What this project is

An analysis of a real-time bidding (RTB) ad-impression dataset from a take-home
assignment: one day (2021-02-01), US only, 249,232 rows and 26 columns, one auction
opportunity per row. The repository answers the brief's three questions and then
goes further.

1. Q1: characterise the dataset (structure, quality, the RTB funnel).
2. Q2: CTR feature selection, a data-justified feature set (an analysis, not a model).
3. Q3: pre-bid pricing, the CPM that wins a `url × ad_slot` X% of the time.
4. Beyond the brief: a CTR classifier and a quantile regressor (`src/models.py`),
   seven extra analysis layers (`src/layers.py`), and hash forensics on the
   obfuscated columns (`src/decode.py`).

The primary deliverable is `notebooks/analysis.ipynb`. Analysis logic lives in
`src/` so it stays testable and reusable; the notebook is generated by
`build_notebook.py` plus the section modules in `notebook_sections/`.

## Domain facts that encode real bugs if ignored

- CTR is only defined on won impressions. `conversion` is 0 for every lost bid by
  construction. Compute CTR on `won_frame(df)`, never over all bids (that gives 3.5%
  instead of the true 9.4%).
- Auction-outcome columns are leakage for CTR. `bid`, `won_bid` and `feedback_bid`
  are produced by or after the auction. Never use them to predict clicks.
- Missingness is structural. Auction-outcome columns are NaN exactly where
  `bidded == 0`; `device_brand` and `device_model` are NaN for desktop. Do not
  impute blindly.
- `country` and `screen_orientation` are constants with zero predictive value.
- Stratify by `auction_type` for anything price related. First-price and
  second-price are different markets (win 33% at a 1.4 CPM median against 90% at
  38 CPM).
- Sentinels: `viewability == -1` and `session_depth == -1` mean "could not
  estimate", not a real value. Use the `*_est` columns for statistics and keep the
  `*_missing` flag.
- Q3 identity: `P(win | bid) = CDF(clearing_price)`, so
  `bid(X) = quantile_X(clearing_price)`. `clearing_price` is `feedback_bid`, the
  price that decided the auction.
- `user_id` is unique on every row. There is no repeat-user or frequency analysis to
  be done on this file.
- The hashes are salted or keyed. Do not spend time trying to reverse them;
  `src/decode.py` records the 36 million failed guesses. The joins between hashed
  columns are intact and are the useful part.

## Conventions

- Python 3.11 or newer (developed on 3.14, Jupyter kernel `nsrb314`). Dependencies
  in `requirements.txt`.
- Analysis functions return tidy DataFrames; plotting lives in `src/layer_plots.py`,
  the `plot_*` functions of `src/models.py`, and the notebook.
- Import from `src/` by adding it to `sys.path` (the notebook does this in its setup
  cell; the scripts do the same).
- Charts: call `viz_style.apply_style()`, assign categorical hues in fixed order, one
  y axis per chart, single-hue sequential ramps. Pick the chart form by the
  relationship it shows (the Visual Vocabulary families) and say why in the
  adjacent markdown.
- Diagrams: built with the diagram-design skill on the repo palette (paper
  `#fcfcfb`, ink `#0b0b0b`, muted `#52514e`, accent `#2a78d6`, link `#eb6834`).
  Orthogonal connectors, masked labels with a 6 to 10 px gap from the stroke, legend
  strip at the bottom, one or two accent elements per diagram. Run the skill's
  `self_check.py` before exporting; export PNG at 2x with Playwright and SVG with the
  font `@import`. The local `.diagram-design` profile marker is git-ignored.
- Prose (README, findings, notebook markdown, docstrings): plain and specific. No em
  or en dashes, no emojis, no bold-header bullet lists, sentence-case headings, no
  filler vocabulary (crucial, pivotal, leverage, robust, landscape, delve, showcase,
  underscore). Numbers over adjectives.
- Images: only openly licensed photos with the licence and source recorded in
  `reports/images/CREDITS.md`, or our own SVG. Nothing of unknown provenance, nothing
  whose subject is third-party branding.

## Working commands

```bash
python3 build_notebook.py                                    # regenerate notebook source
python3 -m jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute \
  --inplace --ExecutePreprocessor.kernel_name=nsrb314 notebooks/analysis.ipynb
python3 scripts/run_models.py                                # ML metrics and figures, about 35 s
python3 scripts/run_layers.py                                # layers and decoding, about 25 s
```

The notebook last ran clean at 83 cells, 25 figures and zero errors. If you change
a section module, rebuild and re-execute before committing.

## Data and privacy

The dataset (`data/data.csv`) and the assignment brief (`Analysis-NSRB.pdf`) belong
to the assignment's author and are git-ignored. Do not commit them or paste raw rows
into public artifacts. Loose images dropped in the project root are ignored too
(`/*.jpg`, `/*.png`, `/*.webp`); only files under `reports/images/` with an entry in
`CREDITS.md` are published.

## If you extend this

- Temporal validation: with more days, train on earlier days and test on later ones.
  Both models currently use a single day.
- Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier or Cox) for censored losses in Q3.
- A per-slot shading policy built from the Q3 quantile curves, to approach the 47%
  saving ceiling measured in the layers section.
- `tests/` for the `src/` functions: funnel counts, IV monotonicity, calibration,
  functional-dependency values.
